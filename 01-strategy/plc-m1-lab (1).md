# Craft an Advanced Product Strategy, Module 1 Lab

## Write your one-page strategy
- **Winning aspiration: define winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics. What does success look like for the specific person you serve?:** Fable is the place I come back to on purpose not because I need rescuing anymore, but because staying steady is easier with it than without it.
- **Where to play: segment, geography, and use case, explicitly. Which customers, markets, and surfaces will you focus on?:** Segment: the maintenance user, not the crisis user. Geography: hold, don't expand
No new geographic markets as part of this initiative. This is a deliberate exclusion, not an oversight. Use case: micro-maintenance, not crisis intervention and not therapy
- **How to win: your differentiator. What can you do that your specific competitors cannot easily replicate?:** Fable is the only product that already knows what "better" looked like for this specific person because it watched them get there.
- **Capabilities required: what you must be world-class at. What will you build, buy, or partner for?:** Behavioral pattern recognition on existing usage data. 2. Privacy and trust architecture. 3. Re-entry UX design coming back without shame.
- **Management systems: the metrics and rituals that keep your choices alive quarter to quarter.:** We track day-90-to-180 return and return sentiment as our primary signals  not DAU and we hold a monthly checkpoint and a clinical veto specifically to stop the team from quietly drifting back into engagement tactics we've explicitly ruled out.

## Name your one hard no
- **Your one hard no:** We will not build the community or social layer the growth team is advocating for even though it would likely move engagement metrics in the short term  because visibility is the opposite of what our highest-value user wants.

## Write your three OKRs
- **Objective:** Become the product our previously-in-crisis users return to on their own terms proving that Fable can win the maintenance phase without relying on the engagement tactics we explicitly ruled out.
- **KR1:** Increase Day 90–180 return rate among users who had high engagement during their first 90 days, from a 22% baseline to 40%, without an increase in push notification volume per user.
- **KR2:** Increase premium subscription retention at the 6-month mark for the previously-high-engagement, now-lapsing cohort from a 48% baseline to 65%.
- **KR3:** Improve post-return session sentiment (measured via in-app micro-survey: "did this feel helpful or like an obligation") from a 61% positive baseline to 80% positive, while return rate (KR1) is simultaneously increasing.

## AI pressure-test
- **Which challenge from the AI is most valid, and why?:** the assumption that "returning with intent" is a real, sizeable behavior, not just the story the strategy needs to be true. It's the only challenge that, if wrong, breaks every other part of the cascade  not just one piece of it.
- **What would you change based on the pushback, and what would you defend?:** I'd add a validation gate before the board ask, not after it. The single most important fix is sequencing: instead of taking this document straight to the board as a funding request, I'd propose the first 6–8 weeks of the initiative as a cheap, sharp test of the core premise — take a small cohort of lapsed, previously-high-engagement users, manually or semi-manually surface a lightweight version of "here's what helped you before" via email or a simple in-app moment, and measure whether any elevated return behavior shows up. Not a full build. Not the personalization engine. Just: does reminding someone of their own recovery pattern produce a detectable lift over doing nothing? If the answer is no, or the effect is tiny, I've saved the team six months and the board a wasted initiative. This turns the board ask from "fund this because the thesis is elegant" into "fund this because we saw an early signal and now need real investment to scale it" a fundamentally stronger position, and it directly answers the CSO's sharpest point: that the strategy is confused for validated when it's only argued.
