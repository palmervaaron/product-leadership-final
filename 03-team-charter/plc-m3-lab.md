## Refine and complete your charter
- **What We Own. What this team owns.:** The maintenance-phase retention outcome. KR1 (day 90–180 return rate), KR2 (6-month premium retention for the lapsing cohort), and KR3 (post-return sentiment). These are this team's numbers to move, full stop.
The strategic direction and roadmap of the AI check-in / behavioral memory engine for as long as it remains this quarter's core Now bet including what it surfaces, when it triggers, and what "remembering the user" means in practice.
Notification reliability as it affects the re-entry moment (timing, timezone accuracy) not the full notification system end-to-end, but the specific failure modes that undermine trust at return.
WCAG 2.1 compliance for the core product experience.
Enforcement of the hard no: no streak mechanics, no visible/identity-linked social features, no engagement tactics that trade short-term lift for the trust this strategy depends on. This team is accountable for catching drift toward these, not just avoiding them ourselves.
- **What is out of scope.:** The first-90-day onboarding flow and its drop-off problem that's PM1's surface and a different OKR (activation, not retention). We don't own fixing the 40% drop-off, even though we're consulted where our data touches it.
Top-of-funnel acquisition and general engagement/growth tactics Growth's domain, bounded by the same hard no this team enforces.
Clinical program design, crisis-mode flows, or anything that reads as a step toward clinical repositioning that requires joint Clinical + Legal review and sits outside this team's authority to greenlight alone.
Geographic expansion, web/platform expansion (Apple Watch, web version), B2B ("Fable for Teams") all explicitly excluded by the strategy's Where to Play, regardless of who's asking.
- **Cross-boundary decisions that need a joint call.:** Any change to the AI check-in engine that touches the onboarding surface where it first appears, what data it captures during a user's acute phase, how early re-entry behavior gets tagged requires sign-off from PM1, since we're operating on their surface even though the outcome belongs to us.
Any nudge, trigger, or check-in design requires the clinical veto before shipping, per the management system, regardless of who proposed it or how much lift it might drive.
Any messaging or notification change that could plausibly be read as an engagement/growth tactic requires a joint review with Growth not to get their approval on strategy, but to make sure we haven't drifted into their old playbook without noticing.
- **How We Decide. Who decides feature and scope calls.:** Within this team: PM2 holds final call on any trade-off that affects KR1–KR3 directly if a design choice trades sentiment for return rate, or vice versa, that decision sits with PM2, because it's their OKR.
On the AI check-in engine specifically: PM3 holds final call on technical architecture and build sequencing. PM3 does not hold final call on whether or how the engine surfaces on the onboarding surface  that's a joint decision with PM1 (see below).
On anything touching the hard no: the clinical veto is absolute and non-negotiable, regardless of whose surface it's on or how much business case exists for an exception. This is not a vote, it's a veto.
- **How cross-team conflicts escalate.:** PM3 is Responsible for building the AI check-in engine.
PM1 and PM2 are jointly Accountable for where and how it surfaces on their respective surfaces (onboarding vs. retention), since each owns the outcome of their own surface.
Growth is Consulted on any engagement-adjacent design choice, given their stake, but does not hold decision rights over this initiative's roadmap.
The product leader (this charter's owner) is Informed of all cross-boundary decisions and is the named escalation point when PM1 and PM3 or PM2 and Growth  can't reach agreement.
- **Who resolves escalations from outside the team.:** First 48 hours: PM1 and PM3 (or whichever two roles are in conflict) attempt to resolve directly, using the strategy's OKRs and the hard no as the shared tiebreaker criteria not seniority, not whoever built the feature.
If unresolved after 48 hours: escalate to the product leader for a decision. The product leader commits to a decision within 3 business days, not an open-ended review.
Decision criteria, in order: (1) does it violate the hard no? if yes, decision is made, no further debate; (2) does it serve KR1–KR3 without compromising the surface owner's own OKR  if there's a genuine trade-off, the product leader decides which OKR takes priority this quarter; (3) if neither applies cleanly, default to the surface owner (PM1 for onboarding, PM2 for retention).
Standing forum, not just ad hoc escalation: recurring tension between PM1/PM3 or Growth/this team gets a standing slot in the existing monthly checkpoint  the same ritual already protecting the hard no  rather than being litigated fresh every time it comes up. If the same conflict surfaces twice in one month, that's a signal the ownership boundary itself needs revisiting, not just the individual decision.
